Skull Crusher

The skull crusher, or lying triceps extension, trains the triceps by lowering an EZ bar toward your head from straight arms and pressing it back. Only the elbows bend. With the upper arms leaning slightly back, the long head of the triceps gets a deep stretch.

How to do the skull crusher

  1. Lie on a flat bench with your feet on the floor and hold an EZ bar over your chest, hands about shoulder width apart.
  2. Lean your upper arms back slightly toward your head and keep them there.
  3. Bend only at the elbows to lower the bar in an arc toward your forehead or just behind your head.
  4. Keep your elbows pointing up, not flaring out to the sides.
  5. Straighten your elbows to bring the bar back up to the start.
  6. Keep your body flat on the bench and your feet planted for every rep.

Form cues and common mistakes

The same coaching points MUSQ shows on the lifter in the app.

Elbow Position

Only the elbows bend.

Why it mattersFixed upper arms make elbow extension the only motion, so the triceps do the work instead of the lats and chest.

Common mistakeElbows flaring wide as the bar lowers, or the upper arms swinging to help press it back up.

Do this insteadKeep the elbows about shoulder-width and pointing at the ceiling for every rep.

Bar Path

The bar travels in an arc toward the head.

Why it mattersLowering the bar to the forehead or just behind it takes the triceps through a long range while keeping the path controlled.

Common mistakeDropping the bar quickly toward the face.

Do this insteadLower the bar slowly to the forehead or just behind the head, then extend the elbows to bring it back.

Arm Angle

The upper arms lean slightly back toward the head.

Why it mattersTilting the upper arms back keeps tension on the triceps at the top, where vertical arms let the bones take the load.

Common mistakeUpper arms vertical at lockout, so the triceps rest between reps.

Do this insteadStart with the arms angled slightly back from vertical and keep that angle throughout.

Body Position

The body stays flat and still on the bench.

Why it mattersKeeping the hips and upper back on the bench prevents arching and keeps the triceps working against the whole load.

Common mistakeLifting the hips or arching the back as the reps get hard.

Do this insteadKeep the head, upper back and hips in contact with the bench throughout.

Base

The feet anchor the body to the floor.

Why it mattersFlat feet give a stable base so the body doesn't shift as the bar moves overhead.

Common mistakeFeet up on the bench edge or on the toes.

Do this insteadPlant both feet flat on the floor, about hip-width apart.

Skull Crusher FAQ

What muscles do skull crushers work?

The triceps, especially the long head, which is stretched because the arms point back over the head. The rear shoulder helps hold the arms in place.

Are skull crushers bad for your elbows?

They can bother sensitive elbows when the weight is heavy. An EZ bar, a controlled tempo and lowering to just behind the head instead of the forehead usually help.

Skull crushers with an EZ bar or dumbbells?

The EZ bar is the classic version and is easier on the wrists than a straight bar; dumbbells let each arm work on its own.
